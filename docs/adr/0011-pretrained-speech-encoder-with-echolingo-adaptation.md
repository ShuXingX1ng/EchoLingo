# Build the first Speech Evaluator on a pretrained speech encoder

EchoLingo's first **Speech Evaluator** will not pretrain a speech encoder from scratch. It will derive **Speech Evidence** from an open pretrained speech encoder, add EchoLingo-trained adaptation using broad licensed speech corpora, and train EchoLingo's own scoring capability from labelled learner speech; this preserves genuine model-training ownership while fitting the available RTX 4070 12 GB and avoiding the data and compute cost of foundation-model pretraining.

The speech encoder, recognition/alignment capability, and Speech Evaluator remain explicit boundaries so each can be validated or replaced independently. EchoLingo owns the adaptation and scoring parameters, while the upstream pretrained weights retain their original identity and licence.

For Read Aloud, the resulting Speech Evidence deliberately has three complementary paths: free ASR captures what an unconstrained recognizer can understand, reference-conditioned alignment locates omissions, insertions, repetitions, word timing, and phoneme timing, and acoustic representations retain pronunciation and prosodic evidence. No one path is treated as the score because free ASR can penalize unfamiliar accents while forced alignment can conceal unintelligible speech by matching it to the expected text.

The first Speech Evaluator will use one shared learned scoring representation with separate heads for utterance pronunciation, fluency, word accuracy, phoneme accuracy, prosody, and completeness. Read Aloud Content remains primarily reference-derived rather than an opaque neural prediction, and the user-facing 10–90 dimensions and task estimate belong to a later Read Aloud calibration policy; raw SpeechOcean label scales are retained during foundational supervised training instead of being relabelled as PTE scores.

Training proceeds as controlled, cumulative experiments rather than one opaque mixture: first establish a reproducible pretrained-encoder-plus-SpeechOcean baseline, then compare Loquacious Small adaptation, and only later compare a deduplicated target-accent increment if one is justified. Candidates are evaluated against the same speaker-independent Speech Evaluation Benchmark so broader data is retained only when it produces measurable benefit.

All splits are speaker-independent. Training speakers provide training and validation material, while the foundational SpeechOcean test set is sealed from parameter, epoch, threshold, and loss-weight selection; a later EchoLingo PTE Read Aloud gold benchmark is separately sealed and does not replace the foundational benchmark.

The first reproducible baseline uses `facebook/wav2vec2-base-960h` as the pinned speech foundation checkpoint. Its CTC outputs support free transcription and reference-conditioned alignment while its hidden states supply acoustic representations, and its size and Apache-2.0 model-card licence fit the local training constraint. Whisper remains an ASR comparison and WavLM a later representation challenger; neither is mixed into the first baseline.

The first scoring experiment freezes the full Wav2Vec2 encoder and trains only the multi-task scoring network. A second candidate may add LoRA or adapters to upper encoder layers, but full encoder fine-tuning is excluded at this data scale; adapted weights are accepted only when they improve the sealed benchmark without materially degrading transcription and alignment evidence.

Phoneme-level scoring is included in the first evaluator because SpeechOcean supplies canonical phoneme sequences and expert 0–2 phoneme labels, but it does not supply phoneme timestamps. An independently validated offline alignment step will locate candidate phoneme spans; only alignments passing quality checks contribute phoneme loss, while sentence and word supervision remain usable when phoneme alignment fails. Alignment output is never treated as the pronunciation label.

The first offline phoneme locator is Montreal Forced Aligner with the `english_us_arpa` acoustic model and dictionary, matching SpeechOcean's ARPA phone inventory. It preprocesses foundational training data on CPU and is quality-filtered because its clean native-read-speech training domain may force incorrect boundaries on severe learner errors; this choice does not bind the later runtime aligner.

Before a PTE-specific gold benchmark exists, user-facing 10–90 values use a monotonic Provisional Score Normalization over retained raw SpeechOcean-scale predictions. The UI labels these as EchoLingo reference evaluations rather than PTE predictions; later PTE calibration replaces only this mapping and task policy, not the foundational evidence model.

The provisional Read Aloud task estimate uses 40% Pronunciation, 40% Oral Fluency, and 20% Content after dimension normalization. Content also gates the result: no valid or English response returns the floor, and responses containing only a small fraction of the reference are capped before aggregation. This is an explicit EchoLingo learning-phase policy, not a claimed Pearson formula, and is replaced by a separately validated PTE Task Calibrator when appropriate labels exist.

The first PTE Task Calibrator is a separate small model trained over frozen foundational predictions and task evidence. It must outperform the provisional policy on an independent PTE-specific benchmark before adoption; joint fine-tuning of the encoder, evaluator, and calibrator is deferred until task-level labels are numerous and diverse enough not to erase foundational speech capabilities.

This creates two explicit delivery stages rather than one blocked programme: SpeechOcean and licensed broad speech support the foundational Speech Evaluator now, while the PTE Task Calibrator starts only after legitimate Read Aloud task-level labels exist. Until then, the transparent provisional policy remains in place and proxy provider scores do not fill the missing-label role.

The first foundational label experiment uses SpeechOcean's published aggregate targets for reproducibility. A later disagreement-aware candidate may learn from the five original expert ratings retained in `scores-detail.json`, but replaces the aggregate baseline only after benchmark improvement; rater disagreement remains internal evaluation data and is not added to the learner-facing response.

Speech data is managed as immutable raw releases, reproducible derived audio/alignment/features, fixed speaker-level split manifests, sealed benchmark material, and a physically separate future consented-user area. Speech Dataset Manifests retain sample hashes, dataset version, source and licence, speaker partition, and consent class; audio, feature caches, and weights remain outside ordinary Git, while every checkpoint records the exact manifests it consumed.

Loquacious Small is the sole broad-speech adaptation pool in the first stage because it already contains Common Voice 18.0 alongside five other sources. Common Voice is not appended independently; any later target-accent increment must exclude Loquacious samples and speakers through source identity, speaker metadata, and audio fingerprint checks and must also be checked against every SpeechOcean partition.

Loquacious adaptation is supervised CTC LoRA or adapter training over its audio-transcript pairs, not pronunciation scoring and not a first-stage restart of Wav2Vec2 self-supervised pretraining. The adapted encoder is then evaluated for ASR and alignment before its mostly frozen representations feed SpeechOcean scoring heads; representation adaptation is retained only if the separate scoring benchmark also improves or yields a justified robustness trade-off.

The first scoring baseline normalises each target scale and averages within each valid hierarchy before applying fixed loss groups: 45% sentence, 30% word, and 25% phoneme. Missing or rejected alignments are masked and long utterances cannot dominate through phone count; later dynamic weighting is a separately benchmarked candidate, and all weight selection uses validation data rather than the sealed test set.

This evaluator programme is the first model priority. Native Speech Model training starts only after the foundational Read Aloud evaluator is trained, accepted, served through its worker, and integrated with comparison diagnostics; future tutoring consumes accepted Speech Evaluation Results and does not create a competing pronunciation score path.

All Evaluator Training Runs are tracked in a local MLflow store with experiment identity, pinned model and data manifests, code revision, seeds, hyperparameters, augmentation and adaptation choices, training curves, multi-metric and subgroup results, duration, GPU identity, and peak memory. MLflow is research-only; only a run that passes acceptance is exported as an immutable Evaluator Checkpoint Bundle for the worker.

Foundational supervised augmentation is limited to mild label-preserving acoustic changes such as gain, background noise, room response, codec effects, and conservative feature masking, with original recordings remaining dominant. Time stretching, pitch shifting, segment deletion, synthetic mispronunciations, and TTS-derived scored examples are excluded because they can change fluency, prosody, completeness, or pronunciation truth; augmentation is retained only when both clean and robustness benchmarks justify it.

The scoring network preserves three temporal levels rather than globally averaging Wav2Vec2 frames: quality-filtered phoneme spans feed phoneme heads, word spans feed word and stress heads, and attentive statistics pooling feeds utterance pronunciation, fluency, prosody, and completeness heads. Explicit pause, duration, speech-rate, repetition, pitch, energy, and timing-stability features are concatenated where relevant so fluency and prosody judgements retain inspectable evidence.

Candidate selection is multi-metric: Pearson and Spearman agreement, mean absolute error and score-band error at sentence level; error and detection metrics at word and phoneme level; ASR error and alignment coverage; clean-to-noisy robustness; and age, gender, and speaker-group slices. No single aggregate correlation authorizes promotion, and a candidate with a material subgroup or evidence-coverage regression must be rejected or carry an explicit justified trade-off.

Adult and child SpeechOcean speakers both contribute foundational supervision, with balanced sampling and separate evaluation slices, but adult performance governs target-domain promotion. An adult-targeted fine-tuning candidate is compared after all-speaker training, age is never supplied as an online scoring feature, and child labels do not enter the future PTE Task Calibrator.

The foundational scoring-validity claim is limited to the Mandarin-L1 learner population represented by SpeechOcean. Loquacious and targeted Common Voice adaptation may broaden representation and ASR robustness but cannot establish fair scoring for other accents without labelled subgroup benchmarks; future PTE Task Labels must deliberately cover target first-language and regional-accent groups.

The personal-learning runtime initially reuses MFA phoneme boundaries to match foundational preprocessing. Successful alignment enables phoneme detail; failed phoneme alignment degrades to sentence and word evaluation, while insufficient sentence or word evidence rejects the evaluation instead of fabricating a plausible score. A lower-latency neural or distilled aligner is a later measured replacement, not a first-baseline dependency.
